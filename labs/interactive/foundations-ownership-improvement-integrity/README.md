# Foundations Ownership and Continuous Improvement Integrity

Reducing clicks is not the same as improving an operating system. The starter incident adds a cron restart that cuts manual restart time, but the payments service still hands production incidents to central Operations, the service team lacks rollback and traffic authority, one expert holds the recovery knowledge, and the underlying connection leak remains technical debt without an owner or exit condition.

Repair `ownership_gate.py` so success is bound to service outcome ownership and verified systemic improvement rather than a completed automation task.

The repaired gate must keep the payments team responsible for the payment outcome; provide bounded rollback, traffic-control, alert-change and reliability-priority decision rights; provide direct runtime evidence and an explicit platform escalation boundary; prove collective recovery capability with multiple trained responders and a runbook validated by someone other than its author; remove the recurring failure mechanism instead of automating its symptom; manage technical debt with owner, risk, interest metric, review date and exit condition; use a 28-day baseline, explicit hypothesis, guardrails, owned action and 28-day evaluation; meet toil, page and payment-success targets; institutionalize the learning in a regression test, runbook, alert and ownership catalog; perform an effectiveness review; and prove a second identical analysis is mutation-free and byte-reproducible.
